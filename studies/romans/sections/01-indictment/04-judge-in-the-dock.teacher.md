# The Judge in the Dock - teacher notes

**Romans 2:1-16**

Preparation, and notes for leading a discussion. Not part of the self-directed
lesson.

### Springing the trap in a group

- The lesson asks the reader to notice, on their own, that they spent chapter 1
  agreeing. In a group you can do better than that, because agreement is audible.
- Let the room agree out loud with chapter 1 first. Ask what they made of the vice
  list, and let the nodding happen without correcting it. Then read 2:1 and let the
  silence do the work. Do not explain the turn before they feel it.
- Resist rescuing anyone from the discomfort. The discomfort is Paul's, it is
  deliberate, and the whole indictment depends on it landing.

### If the group deflects

- The common escape is to agree that judging is bad in general, which converts a
  personal indictment into a shared principle and costs nothing. Push back toward
  the specific: not "we all judge" but "which sins are easy for me to condemn, and
  why those."
- The second escape is to jump ahead to grace. Chapter 3 is coming; it is worth
  saying that it is coming, and then staying here.

### A question that works

Asked after 2:1 has landed, and not before: *whose sin were you picturing while we
read chapter 1?*
