# The Golden Chain - teacher notes

**Romans 8:29-30**

Preparation, not lesson content. The room needs the three sentences in the
lesson; it does not need the vocabulary below unless somebody brings it.

### If someone raises the order of the decrees

They may, and the answer is not to pick a side in that debate.

- Both of the standard positions are framed impersonally. Each orders *decrees* —
  items in a plan arranged on a logical line, as though the question were the
  sequence of entries in a ledger. The debate was guarding something real, the
  priority of grace and the sovereignty of God, and it is not a foolish question. But
  its frame abstracts the decree away from the One decreeing.
- **Paul supplies his own ordering in this very passage, and it is personal from the
  first word.** He does not begin with a decree about a category. He begins with
  *whom He foreknew*. The chain is an account of what God does with people, not of the
  order in which He resolved propositions.
- So the study follows Paul's ordering and does not need to adjudicate the other. The
  relational reading fits the chain better than either ordering does, whichever one it
  ends up leaning toward.
- **The deeper reason is who God is.** A Triune God is relational in His own being —
  Father, Son and Spirit in love before anything was made. John 17:24 has the Father
  loving the Son before the foundation of the world, so that phrase is not an empty
  point at the start of a timeline; it is already full of relationship. An account of
  election that drops the relational aspect describes a lesser God than the one who is
  there.
- 1 Peter 1:2 is the text to reach for: election stated with all three persons in a
  single breath. Scripture's own way of stating election is Trinitarian and personal.

### If someone hears love as a demotion of glory

- The worry is that grounding election in love displaces the display of God's glory.
  It does not. **His love is one of the riches His glory consists of.**
- Romans 5:8 says the demonstration of love *is* the display. Exodus 33:18-19 settles
  it from God's own mouth: Moses asks to see His glory, and God answers by proclaiming
  Himself merciful, gracious and abounding in steadfast love. Glory is the category;
  love is part of what is in it.
- Do not let the room hear love and glory as a warm answer and a severe one that have
  to be balanced. There is one answer.

### Two objections to expect

- *How can God love us before we exist?* The persons are determinate to Him before
  they are actual to us. This is why the language is personal — *whom* He foreknew —
  and it is the same assumption Jeremiah 1:5 and Psalm 139:16 already make.
- *Does this make evil necessary?* The history that produced us is the history He
  chose in order to have us. Do not soften that into evil being an accident He worked
  around; say instead that He judged us worth it, and that Romans 8:18 has already
  said the coming glory outweighs the cost.
