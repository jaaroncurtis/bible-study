# Romans: Study Pacing

Forty lessons through the letter, sequenced for teaching rather than for reading.
`outline.md` maps what Paul is *arguing*; this maps what we *do*, week by week.

Every lesson carries an **aim** in one sentence, the **content** to work through, and
**reflection** that turns it on the person in the room. The balance shifts as the
letter does — chapters 1-11 carry more content than reflection, chapters 12-16 invert
that.

## Method: Romans is the spine, not the boundary

The lessons lean hard on scripture interpreting scripture. Romans supplies the
sequence and the argument; the themes it raises get chased aggressively across the
canon. A lesson is not finished when Romans has been explained — it is finished when
the theme has been followed to where scripture develops it most clearly.

As each lesson is fleshed out it gains a **Cross-references** block listing the
passages it pulls in and what each is there to do. Those blocks appear below only
where the reading is already decided; the rest are filled in as we work.

## Reading "Jew and Gentile" in chapters 2-4

In the indictment, Paul's two groups are not two ethnicities to a modern reader —
they are two postures, and the letter only lands if we name them as such.

- The **Jew** of chapters 2-4 is the one who holds the standard: religious, taught,
  morally serious, in possession of God's law and broadly aligned to it, and
  confident of his standing because of it.
- The **Gentile** is the outsider: worldly, immoral, without the standard and not
  pretending to have it.

Put plainly for today: **the unregenerate visible church versus the world.** That is
the pairing an American Christian needs, because it reproduces the experience a
first-century Roman reader actually had. Paul spends chapter 1 cataloguing the
world's corruption while the religious reader nods along — and then 2:1 turns the
indictment on the one nodding. A congregation that reads chapter 1 as being about the
culture and chapter 2 as being about them has read Romans correctly.

**This reading stops at the end of chapter 4.** When chapters 9-11 arrive, *Israel*
means national, ethnic Israel and God's covenant faithfulness to that people. Do not
carry the chapter-2 cipher into it. If "Israel" there is read as shorthand for
hypocritical religious people, Paul's actual question — whether God has abandoned a
people He bound Himself to — becomes unaskable, and 11:1 stops making sense. The
guardrail is restated where Part Four begins.

## The through-lines

Five commitments run the length of the study. They are stated here once so each
lesson can lean on them without re-explaining:

- **Righteousness is ought-ness.** We are righteous when we are what we were created
  to be. Unrighteousness is deviation from that.
- **God holds the royal prerogative** — the exclusive right to define good and evil.
  The tree stands in for that right.
- **Man was made a vassal king** — genuinely ruling, under total allegiance to the
  King of Kings.
- **Sin is our own righteousness apart from God.** Having seized the right to define
  ought-ness, we must serve the counterfeit definition we produced.
- **A *doulos* is owned.** Autonomy was never on the menu; the only live question is
  which master.

# Part One: The Accession and the Indictment

*Romans 1:1-3:20 — the King is announced, and the court is convened.*

## Lesson 1 — The *Doulos* and the Gospel

**Romans 1:1-17**

**Aim.** Put the study's governing words on the table, and let people notice what
those words are already doing in their thinking.

Deliberately open-ended. We are not settling anything this week.

**Content**

- Paul's self-designation. What *doulos* could mean across the ancient Greco-Roman
  world Paul wrote into — the full range available to his first readers, not a
  single dictionary gloss.
- How each of those possible senses changes what Paul is claiming about himself.
- Paul's "obligations" — what he says he owes, and to whom.
- The gospel as Paul states it here: an announcement about a person, not first a
  procedure for being saved.

**Reflection**

On wearing the word himself:

- How does it feel to apply *doulos* to myself?
- What do I like about it? What bothers me?
- Where does my actual thinking and decision-making line up with this, and where
  does it quietly deviate?

On three words the letter runs on — **gospel**, **righteousness**, **faith**:

- What do I understand each to mean?
- What does each actually mean to me day to day, as opposed to on paper?
- How do those working definitions shape what I decide?

Drawing it together:

- Can I see a connection between being a *doulos* of Christ and what I mean by
  gospel, righteousness, and faith?

## Lesson 2 — Honor Him as God

**Romans 1:18-21a**

**Aim.** Establish who God is and what honoring Him *as God* requires, because
everything the letter later argues about righteousness rests on it.

**Content**

- What do we even mean by "god" in the abstract? And on what grounds is Jehovah *the
  one true God* rather than one candidate among them?
- The tree of the knowledge of good and evil as God's **royal prerogative** — His
  exclusive right to define good and evil.
- Man created as **vassal king**: real rule, real authority, held under total
  allegiance.

**Cross-references**

- **Genesis 1:26-28** — the commission that makes man a ruler in the first place.
- **Genesis 2:15-17** — the one withheld tree; rule granted, prerogative retained.
- **Genesis 3:1-7** — the seizure, read here for what it claims rather than for its
  consequences, which Lesson 3 takes up.
- **Righteousness as ought-ness.** Righteous is being what we were made to be;
  unrighteous is deviating from it.
- Therefore honoring God as God is the *prerequisite* to righteousness, and it means
  total submission to His definition of ought-ness.
- To be righteous is to reclaim the post we abandoned: loyal vassal king under the
  King of Kings.

**Reflection**

- Whose definition of good and evil am I actually operating on this week?
- Where do I want the authority of a king without the allegiance of a vassal?

## Lesson 3 — The Fall

**Romans 1:21b-32**

**Aim.** Trace what the seizure of the royal prerogative actually did to us, and what
God's response to it is.

This is the lesson that goes into the prophets, because Paul is already there: the
idolatry indictment of Romans 1:21b-23 is built out of theirs.

**Content**

- **The declaration of war.** In taking the knowledge of good and evil, we took God's
  place in defining ought-ness. We set out to define our own righteousness.
- **The new master.** We were made to serve. Having refused the true Master, we did
  not become free — we needed another, and the only one on offer was the broken,
  perverted definition of righteousness we ourselves produced. Broken because we were
  never built with the capacity to define it.
- **This is sin**: our own righteousness apart from God. Not merely bad acts — a rival
  standard, and a master.
- **The judgment is the handover.** God "gives us over." The sentence is being left to
  the master we chose.
- **Futility as curse.** Unable to be what we were made to be, we are futile.
- **Reduced providence.** Having rejected God, we are cursed with less of His
  provision, thrown back to some real degree on our own effort to sustain ourselves.

**Cross-references**

The prophetic material, because Paul's language here is quoted from it:

- **Psalm 106:19-20** and **Jeremiah 2:11** — exchanging glory for an image. Paul's
  verb in 1:23 is theirs; this is the charge he is re-filing against everyone.
- **Jeremiah 2:5** — they went after worthlessness and became worthless. Futility as
  the prophets state it: you become like what you serve.
- **Psalm 115:4-8** and **Psalm 135:15-18** — idols with eyes that do not see, and
  makers who become like them. The mechanism of futility, stated plainly.
- **Isaiah 44:9-20** — half the log burned for warmth, half carved into a god. The
  fullest exposure of what the exchange actually is.
- **Jeremiah 2:13** — the two evils: forsaking the fountain, and hewing cisterns that
  hold no water. Reduced providence in the prophets' own image.

For the seizure itself:

- **Genesis 3:1-7** — carried over from Lesson 2, now read for what it produced.
- **Genesis 3:16-19** — the ground cursed, thorns, sweat. Where reduced providence
  comes from; Romans 1 gives the handover, Genesis gives the conditions.

**Reflection**

- Where am I still trying to define righteousness for myself?
- Can I name a place where being "given over" to my own standard has felt like
  freedom?
- Where do I feel the reduced providence — and how do I usually respond to it?

## Lesson 4 — The Judge in the Dock

**Romans 2:1-16**

**Aim.** Close the escape hatch the previous lesson opens, where the religious person
reads chapter 1 as being about somebody else.

**Content**

- **Spring the trap deliberately.** Chapter 1 was a catalogue of the world's
  corruption, and the room has been agreeing with it for two lessons. 2:1 is aimed at
  the agreeing. Teach it that way: let people notice they were nodding.
- The turn — the one passing judgment condemns himself, because he does the same
  things. Not similar things. The same.
- God's kindness meant to lead to repentance, not to be read as a verdict already
  returned in our favour.
- Judgment according to works, without partiality. The insider's standing buys no
  leniency; if anything the standard he holds raises the bar.
- The Gentile who does by nature what the law requires: the outsider sometimes keeps
  what the insider only teaches. Conscience as evidence that ought-ness is written
  in, not invented.

**Reflection**

- Which sins do I find it easy to condemn, and what does the ease tell me?
- Do I read God's patience with me as approval?
- Where is someone outside the church quietly doing what I only talk about?

## Lesson 5 — Circumcision of the Heart

**Romans 2:17-29**

**Aim.** Show that possessing the standard is not the same as keeping it, and that
the marker without the reality is nothing. This is the lesson where the chapter-2
reframing does its hardest work.

**Content**

- **Read Paul's "if you call yourself a Jew" as "if you call yourself a Christian."**
  Every item in his list transfers intact: relying on the standard, boasting in God,
  knowing His will, approving what is excellent because you have been instructed. That
  is a churchgoing, Bible-literate, morally serious person — and Paul is about to
  charge him.
- The boast turned into the charge: you who teach another, do you not teach yourself?
  The teaching role is not evidence of the reality; it can be a substitute for it.
- God's name blasphemed among the outsiders **because of** those who carry it. The
  world's contempt for the church is, at least sometimes, earned and diagnostic.
- The marker versus the reality. For Paul, circumcision; for us, the visible
  equivalents — baptism, membership, attendance, the right positions, fluency in the
  vocabulary. All of them real, none of them the thing itself.
- The one who is one *inwardly*, by the Spirit rather than the letter.
- Praise from God rather than from man.

**Reflection**

- Where am I trading on the marker — the label, the role, the reputation — instead of
  the reality?
- Whose praise am I actually working for?
- If an outsider judged Christ by watching me, what would they conclude?

## Lesson 6 — Does Our Failure Undo God?

**Romans 3:1-8**

**Aim.** Handle the objections head-on, because the study will meet them again in
chapter 9.

**Content**

- What advantage, then? Having levelled the insider with the outsider, Paul refuses
  the conclusion that the standard was therefore worthless. Much advantage, in every
  way — the oracles were entrusted to them.
- Worth naming for us: the visible church really does hold something the world does
  not. The indictment of Lesson 5 does not cancel that, and Paul will not let it.
- Human faithlessness does not nullify God's faithfulness.
- The objection that if our sin displays God's righteousness He is unjust to judge —
  and Paul's refusal to soften it.
- "Let us do evil that good may come" named as slander.

**Reflection**

- Do I quietly assume my failure is bigger than God's commitment?
- Where do I use grace as a licence rather than receive it as rescue?

## Lesson 7 — None Righteous

**Romans 3:9-20**

**Aim.** Reach the verdict the whole indictment has been building toward, and see
what the law was actually for.

**Content**

- Both groups under sin — the religious and the worldly arrive at the same verdict by
  different roads. Two lessons of levelling reach their conclusion here.
- The catena of Old Testament charges: the whole person, itemised. Note that Paul
  indicts the insider **out of the insider's own scriptures**.
- Every mouth stopped, the whole world accountable. Not "most mouths."
- Through the law comes knowledge of sin — the law as diagnostic, never as cure.
- Tie back: if righteousness is ought-ness, the law is the published specification we
  cannot meet.

**Reflection**

- Do I actually believe the verdict applies to me without qualification?
- What do I reach for when I want to be told I am doing fine?
- Which road did I take to the same verdict — respectability or open rebellion?

# Part Two: The Purchase

*Romans 3:21-5:21 — righteousness conferred, and a new head over the race.*

## Lesson 8 — Righteousness Apart from Law

**Romans 3:21-26**

**Aim.** Turn the corner of the letter, and see how God stays just while justifying.

**Content**

- A righteousness of God manifested apart from the law, yet witnessed by it.
- Justified as a gift, through the redemption — *apolytrōsis*, the buying out.
- The slave-market image made doctrine: bought, and therefore owned by the buyer.
- Propitiation, and God's passing over former sins — the problem of how He can acquit
  without ceasing to be just.
- Just and the justifier: both halves held at once.

**Reflection**

- If I was bought, what follows about what I now am?
- Do I want God's mercy at the cost of His justice?

## Lesson 9 — Boasting Excluded

**Romans 3:27-31**

**Aim.** Show why the mechanism of justification rules out the very thing the flesh
wants from it.

**Content**

- On what principle is boasting excluded? Not works — faith.
- One God, therefore one way, for insider and outsider alike. The religious person
  does not get a second, more dignified route in.
- Do we then overthrow the law? On the contrary, we uphold it.

**Reflection**

- Where does my boasting go when the obvious route is closed?
- What would it look like to have nothing to contribute and be glad of it?
- Do I secretly expect my churchmanship to count for something at the end?

## Lesson 10 — Abraham Credited

**Romans 4:1-12**

**Aim.** Ground justification by faith in the patriarch, before the law and before
the marker.

**Content**

- Abraham believed, and it was counted to him — counted, not earned.
- Wages versus gift: the accounting language and why Paul chose it.
- The blessing of the one against whom God will not count sin.
- The sequence that settles it: credited *before* circumcision, so the marker follows
  the reality rather than producing it. This is Lesson 5's point proved from the
  patriarch — and it closes the chapter-2 reframing, because Abraham was reckoned
  righteous while still, by the later marker, an outsider.

**Reflection**

- Am I working for wages or receiving a gift? How would I tell from the inside?
- Which of my markers am I treating as though it produced the reality?

## Lesson 11 — The Faith That Receives

**Romans 4:13-25**

**Aim.** Take Abraham's faith apart, and name its parts.

**Content**

- The promise comes through the righteousness of faith, not through the law.
- Abraham's faith examined through **notitia** (he knew what was promised),
  **assensus** (he judged God able), and **fiducia** (he rested his life on it).
- Faith as the empty hand: it contributes nothing and receives everything.
- Fully convinced God was able to do what He promised — and this written for our sake
  too.

**Reflection**

- Which of the three do I have? Which am I missing?
- Where do knowledge and agreement sit in me without any resting on them?

## Lesson 12 — Peace and Standing

**Romans 5:1-11**

**Aim.** Show what justification actually produces, including in suffering.

**Content**

- Peace with God, and access into this standing in which we stand.
- Rejoicing in suffering; the chain from endurance to character to hope.
- God's love poured into our hearts by the Spirit.
- The argument from the greater to the lesser: if while enemies we were reconciled by
  His death, how much more shall we be saved by His life.

**Reflection**

- Do I experience my standing as settled or as under review?
- What does my response to suffering reveal about which one I believe?

## Lesson 13 — Two Heads

**Romans 5:12-21**

**Aim.** Establish that we are never masterless or headless — only ever under Adam or
under Christ.

**Content**

- Sin entering through one man, and death spreading to all.
- Adam as a type of the one to come: the structure of representation.
- The asymmetry Paul insists on — not merely restoration but abundance, "much more."
- Where sin increased, grace abounded all the more; grace reigning through
  righteousness.
- Tie back: the vassal-king post abandoned under one head, restored under the other.

**Reflection**

- Do I think of myself as standing alone before God, or as standing under a head?
- What changes if my status is inherited rather than achieved?

# Part Three: The New Reign

*Romans 6:1-8:39 — what it means to be owned by this Master.*

## Lesson 14 — Died to Sin

**Romans 6:1-14**

**Aim.** Answer the first objection to grace by showing that the believer's
relationship to sin has changed in kind, not in degree.

**Content**

- Shall we continue in sin that grace may abound? The question grace always provokes.
- Baptised into His death, buried with Him, raised to walk in newness of life.
- The old self crucified so that the body of sin might be brought to nothing.
- "Consider yourselves dead to sin" — reckoning as acting on what is already true.
- Present your members to God; sin shall not have dominion, for you are not under law
  but under grace.

**Reflection**

- Where am I fighting sin as a subject of it rather than as someone transferred out
  from under it?

## Lesson 15 — Which Master?

**Romans 6:15-23**

**Aim.** The centre of the *doulos* thread: establish that the choice is never
whether to be owned.

**Content**

- Paul's premise: you present yourselves to someone, and whoever that is owns you.
- Therefore autonomy is not one of the options. The only live question is which
  master.
- Slaves of sin leading to death, or slaves of obedience leading to righteousness.
- Freedom here is **transfer**, not self-determination.
- Paul's own aside that he speaks in human terms because of the weakness of their
  flesh — the apostle marking the limits of his own metaphor. Worth weighing against
  the temptation to press "slave" flatly everywhere.
- The wage of sin versus the free gift of God — payment against present.

**Reflection**

- I present myself to something daily. To what?
- Which of my "free choices" are actually the preferences of a master I did not
  choose?

## Lesson 16 — Released to Another

**Romans 7:1-6**

**Aim.** Explain the legal mechanism of the transfer, so freedom from law is not
mistaken for lawlessness.

**Content**

- The law binds a person as long as he lives; the marriage analogy.
- Death dissolves the claim — you have died to the law through the body of Christ.
- Released in order to belong to another, to bear fruit for God.
- Serving in the new way of the Spirit, not the old way of the written code.

**Reflection**

- Released *to whom*? Do I treat freedom as a destination or as a transfer?

## Lesson 17 — The Commandment and the Wanting

**Romans 7:7-13**

**Aim.** Show the law's diagnostic function, and see Genesis 3 replay in the first
person.

**Content**

- Is the law sin? By no means — but Paul would not have known coveting apart from it.
- **Epithymia**: the interior wanting that precedes the reaching.
- The sequence — prohibition, desire aroused by the prohibition, transgression, death
  — is the Genesis 3 pattern retold in the first person. The "see, desire, take" shape
  is visible in it.
- Sin seizing its opportunity *through* the commandment: the standard did not create
  the corruption, it made it show itself.
- The law is holy, righteous and good. The verdict falls on us.

**Reflection**

- Where does a prohibition make me want the thing more? What does that reveal about
  who is actually in charge?

## Lesson 18 — Knowing Without Power

**Romans 7:14-25**

**Aim.** Expose the insufficiency of knowledge and agreement on their own — the
*notitia* and *assensus* of a man with no power behind them.

**Content**

- I do not do what I want, and I do the very thing I hate.
- Agreeing that the law is good while being unable to keep it — assent without
  ability.
- The war between the law of the mind and the law of sin in the members.
- "Who will deliver me?" — the cry the whole chapter exists to produce, and its
  answer.

**Reflection**

- Where am I relying on knowing better to make me do better?
- Have I ever asked the question of verse 24 as a real question?

## Lesson 19 — No Condemnation

**Romans 8:1-11**

**Aim.** Deliver the relief chapter 7 has made necessary, and name what the Spirit
actually does.

**Content**

- No condemnation for those in Christ Jesus.
- What the law could not do, weakened by the flesh, God did.
- The mind set on the flesh versus the mind set on the Spirit — death against life and
  peace.
- The Spirit of Him who raised Jesus dwelling in you: the power absent in chapter 7,
  supplied.

**Reflection**

- Do I live as someone under no condemnation, or as someone awaiting sentencing?

## Lesson 20 — From Slave to Son to Heir

**Romans 8:12-17**

**Aim.** Follow the trajectory past ownership into sonship and inheritance — without
losing either end of it.

**Content**

- Led by the Spirit of God: these are sons of God.
- You did not receive a spirit of slavery to fall back into fear, but of adoption.
- Paul's own qualification of the slave language — the same move John 15:15 makes.
  Hold this alongside chapter 6 rather than letting either cancel the other.
- *Abba*, and the Spirit's witness with our spirit.
- Heirs of God, fellow heirs with Christ — the purchased slave now standing to
  inherit.

**Reflection**

- Do I relate to God as owned, as adopted, or as an heir — and can all three be true
  at once?
- What fear still operates in me that adoption should have ended?

## Lesson 21 — Groaning and the Chain

**Romans 8:18-30**

**Aim.** Set present suffering inside the scope of what God is completing.

**Content**

- Present sufferings not worth comparing with the glory to be revealed.
- Creation subjected to futility, in hope — the curse of Lesson 3 placed on a
  timeline with an end.
- Creation groaning, we groaning, the Spirit interceding.
- All things working together for good, for those called according to purpose.
- Foreknew, predestined, called, justified, glorified — the chain with no dropped
  links.

**Reflection**

- Where do I read futility as permanent when Paul reads it as temporary?
- Does "all things" include the thing I am currently carrying?

## Lesson 22 — Nothing Separates

**Romans 8:31-39**

**Aim.** Let the doctrinal half of the letter land as assurance before the turn to
Israel.

**Content**

- If God is for us, who can be against us?
- He who did not spare His own Son — the argument from the greater gift to every
  lesser one.
- Who shall bring a charge? Who condemns? Christ interceding.
- The list of things that cannot separate, and the deliberate exhaustiveness of it.

**Reflection**

- What do I secretly believe could separate me?
- Is my assurance resting on my grip on Christ or His on me?

# Part Four: The Faithfulness of God

*Romans 9:1-11:36 — has the gospel left God's word to Israel in ruins?*

> **Change the reading here — do not merely drop it.** Through chapters 2-4 we read
> Paul's "Jew" as the religious insider and applied it to the unregenerate visible
> church. Part Four needs a different and more careful move.
>
> **Israel still means Israel.** In these three chapters it is national, ethnic
> Israel — a people God bound Himself to by covenant, whose unbelief is the problem
> Paul is actually wrestling with. Substitute the referent and the argument collapses:
> Paul's grief in 9:1-3 becomes theatrical, "has God rejected His people?" in 11:1
> stops being a real question, the olive tree has no natural branches to break off,
> and the irrevocable gifts and calling of 11:29 have nobody to be irrevocable to.
>
> **And precisely because it is a real people, it is the worked example.** Israel is
> where God's dealings are visible across centuries, and what they show is a
> distinction that holds in every age: **the visible covenant community is not
> identical to the elect within it.** Paul says so outright in 9:6 — not all who are
> descended from Israel belong to Israel — and the remnant of 11:5 is that same
> distinction with a headcount. This is the **visible church and the invisible
> church**, stated in Paul's own terms, demonstrated on a nation.
>
> Hold the difference precisely. We are **not** saying Israel is a code word for the
> church. We are saying that a real, chosen, covenanted people, most of whom did not
> believe, is history's clearest demonstration of how God has always worked — and
> that the same distinction runs through any congregation reading this letter. The
> first reading replaces Israel; this one learns from her.
>
> Say the change out loud when Part Four opens. Six lessons have trained the room to
> hear "Jew" as "us"; without deliberate untraining they will carry the cipher in and
> misread the hardest section of the letter.

## Lesson 23 — The Word Has Not Failed

**Romans 9:1-13**

**Aim.** Feel the weight of the problem before reaching for the answer.

**Content**

- Paul's unceasing anguish; his willingness to be accursed for his kinsmen.
- The privileges that belonged to Israel, listed without irony.
- **9:6 is the hinge of the whole section.** Not all who are descended from Israel
  belong to Israel. Paul's answer to "has the word failed?" is that it never promised
  what the objection assumes — the promise, not the bloodline, defines the line.
- This is the **visible church and the invisible church** in Paul's own words, before
  the vocabulary existed. A covenant community with real membership, real markers and
  real privileges, inside which a smaller company actually belongs to God.
- Isaac and Ishmael, Jacob and Esau: chosen before either had done anything. The line
  runs by call, not by descent and not by performance.

**Reflection**

- Do I grieve over anyone the way Paul grieves here?
- What do I assume God owes me on the basis of belonging?
- If membership in the visible people has never been the same as belonging, what am I
  resting on?

## Lesson 24 — The Potter

**Romans 9:14-29**

**Aim.** Meet the hardest form of the royal-prerogative question: God's right to
define, applied to persons.

**Content**

- Is there injustice on God's part? Paul's answer is a denial, not an explanation.
- Mercy and hardening; Pharaoh raised up for a purpose.
- "Who are you, O man, to answer back to God?" — the potter and the clay.
- This is the royal prerogative at its sharpest. The objection in the text is the
  objection in the room, and it is the Genesis 3 grasp reappearing as theology: the
  demand to hold God to *our* definition of just.
- Vessels of mercy prepared for glory, called from Jews and Gentiles both.

**Reflection**

- When I object here, am I objecting as a creature or as a rival?
- Can I distinguish "I do not understand this" from "I do not accept His right"?

## Lesson 25 — The Stumbling Stone

**Romans 9:30-10:4**

**Aim.** Name precisely what went wrong, in the study's own vocabulary.

**Content**

- Gentiles who did not pursue righteousness attained it; Israel pursuing a law of
  righteousness did not arrive.
- Why? Because they pursued it as though by works.
- Zeal for God, but not according to knowledge.
- Being ignorant of the righteousness of God, they sought to establish their own —
  this is **sin as defined in Lesson 3**, in its most religious possible form.
- Christ as the end of the law for righteousness to everyone who believes.

**Reflection**

- Where is my zeal running ahead of my knowledge?
- What is my most respectable attempt to establish my own righteousness?

## Lesson 26 — Confess and Believe

**Romans 10:5-17**

**Aim.** Hold together the confession of lordship and the belief of the heart, and
see how faith arrives.

**Content**

- The word is near you, in your mouth and in your heart.
- Confessing Jesus as **Lord** and believing God raised Him — allegiance and trust in
  one breath, not two separate requirements.
- The same Lord over all, bestowing riches on all who call.
- The chain backwards: calling, believing, hearing, preaching, sending.
- Faith comes by hearing, and hearing through the word of Christ.

**Reflection**

- Do I treat "Lord" as a title or as a claim on my decisions?
- What am I listening to most, and what faith is it producing?

## Lesson 27 — Did They Not Hear?

**Romans 10:18-11:10**

**Aim.** Close off the excuses, and introduce the remnant.

**Content**

- Their voice has gone out to all the earth — they heard.
- A disobedient and contrary people; God found by those who did not seek Him.
- Has God rejected His people? By no means — Paul himself is evidence.
- Elijah and the seven thousand: the remnant chosen by grace. Elijah counted one
  believer in Israel and was wrong by seven thousand — the invisible church is not
  visible *to us* either, including when we are counting.
- The remnant is 9:6 with a headcount. Israel is not failing to be Israel; the Israel
  within Israel is exactly what God said it would be.
- Grace and works as mutually exclusive principles.
- Hardening, and eyes that do not see.

**Reflection**

- Where do I mistake a small remnant for a failed promise?
- Elijah misjudged by seven thousand. What am I concluding from a headcount I cannot
  actually take?

## Lesson 28 — Do Not Boast

**Romans 11:11-24**

**Aim.** Apply the whole argument to Gentile presumption, which is the reader's own
danger.

**Content**

- Their trespass meaning salvation for the Gentiles, to make Israel jealous.
- The olive tree: branches broken off, a wild shoot grafted in.
- "Do not be arrogant toward the branches" — you do not support the root.
- Broken off for unbelief, standing fast through faith. Note the fear Paul commends.
- If God did not spare the natural branches, neither will He spare you.
- **Membership in the tree is visible; the warning proves it.** A branch that can be
  broken off was really in the tree in some sense — that is the visible church, and
  the warning is addressed to it. Set this alongside Lesson 29: the same chapter that
  breaks branches off calls God's gifts and calling irrevocable, and only the
  visible/invisible distinction lets both stand.

**Reflection**

- Where am I arrogant about a position I did not earn and do not sustain?
- Paul commends fear here. What would appropriate fear look like in me this week, and
  how is it different from doubt?

## Lesson 29 — Unsearchable

**Romans 11:25-36**

**Aim.** End the doctrinal half where Paul ends it — in worship rather than
resolution.

**Content**

- The partial hardening, until the fullness of the Gentiles comes in.
- **The gifts and the calling of God are irrevocable** — the section's load-bearing
  claim, and the study's clearest statement of **perseverance**. What God's call
  establishes, God does not revoke. He does not begin a work and abandon it, and the
  security of the called does not rest on the called.
- **Hold the apparent contradiction and resolve it, do not dodge it.** Branches were
  broken off in Lesson 28; here the calling is irrevocable. Both are in the same
  chapter. They cohere only on the distinction 9:6 established: the branches broken
  off were in the visible community, while the calling that cannot be revoked is the
  effectual call of God to His own. Israel is the worked example of exactly this —
  a covenant people, most of whom did not believe, and a remnant God never lost.
- Carry Lesson 21 back in deliberately: foreknew, predestined, called, justified,
  glorified is the same doctrine stated of individuals, with no dropped links. 8:29-30
  is the stronger proof-text for personal perseverance; 11:29 shows the same
  faithfulness worked out on a nation over centuries, where it can be watched.
- God consigning all to disobedience that He may have mercy on all.
- The doxology: the depth of His riches and wisdom, His unsearchable judgments.
- "Who has known the mind of the Lord?" — the royal prerogative confessed rather than
  contested. Set this beside Lesson 24 deliberately.
- From Him and through Him and to Him are all things.

**Cross-references**

- **Romans 8:29-30** — carried back from Lesson 21. The chain applied to persons; the
  primary ground for perseverance, with 11:29 as its demonstration in history.
- **Romans 9:6** — the distinction this whole section runs on, restated now that the
  argument has landed.
- **Romans 11:5** — the remnant, as the visible/invisible distinction with a number
  attached.

**Reflection**

- Can I let a doctrine end in worship rather than in a settled position?
- If the calling is irrevocable, what does that change about how I hold my own
  standing — and about how I pray for someone who has walked away?

# Part Five: Owned People at Work

*Romans 12:1-15:13 — submission made visible.*

## Lesson 30 — The Living Sacrifice

**Romans 12:1-2**

**Aim.** Work the hinge of the letter, where everything owed is turned into everything
offered.

**Content**

- "Therefore, by the mercies of God" — the ethics run on the doctrine, in that order.
- Present your bodies as a living sacrifice: the same verb as Romans 6, now aimed
  where it belongs.
- Your spiritual, reasonable service — the word is worship and it is also duty.
- Do not be conformed; be transformed by the renewal of the mind.
- Testing and discerning the will of God: **the reclaimed vassal king learning the
  King's definition of ought-ness instead of issuing his own.** This is Lesson 2
  arriving as practice.

**Reflection**

- What is currently forming my mind, and did I choose it?
- Where do I still want to discern God's will as an advisor rather than receive it as
  a subject?

## Lesson 31 — Sober Judgment

**Romans 12:3-8**

**Aim.** Apply the renewed mind first to self-assessment.

**Content**

- Do not think of yourself more highly than you ought — the measure of faith God has
  assigned.
- One body, many members, and members of one another.
- Gifts differing according to grace: use them.

**Reflection**

- Do I know my actual measure, or an inflated and a deflated version I switch between?

## Lesson 32 — Love Without Pretence

**Romans 12:9-21**

**Aim.** Work the texture of ordinary Christian conduct, and hand back the bench.

**Content**

- Love genuine, evil abhorred, good held fast — rapid-fire imperatives, worth slowing
  down.
- Bless those who persecute; weep and rejoice with others; associate with the lowly.
- Never avenge yourselves — leave it to the wrath of God.
- **Vengeance as an abdicated bench**: refusing revenge is refusing to occupy the
  judgment seat we seized in Genesis 3. "Vengeance is mine" is the royal prerogative
  again, and here obedience means handing it back.
- Overcome evil with good.

**Reflection**

- Where am I still sitting on a bench that is not mine?
- Who am I currently judging in place of God?

## Lesson 33 — Governing Authorities

**Romans 13:1-7**

**Aim.** See submission worked out where it costs something, in the study's own
vassal framework.

**Content**

- Every person subject to the governing authorities; no authority except from God.
- The authority as God's servant for good, bearing the sword.
- Subjection for the sake of conscience, not only of wrath.
- Taxes, revenue, respect, honour — owed and paid.
- **Vassal logic.** A delegated authority is real and is to be honoured, precisely
  because it is delegated. This is how a vassal king treats another vassal, without
  confusing him for the King.

**Reflection**

- Where does my submission to delegated authority reveal what I think about the King?
- Where do I use the failings of an authority as a reason to keep the prerogative
  myself?

## Lesson 34 — Love Fulfils the Law

**Romans 13:8-14**

**Aim.** Bring the law back as fulfilled rather than reimposed, and set the clock
running.

**Content**

- Owe nothing except to love; love as the fulfilling of the law.
- The commandments summed up in one.
- Salvation nearer than when we believed; the night far gone.
- Cast off works of darkness, put on the armour of light.
- Put on the Lord Jesus Christ, and make no provision for the flesh to gratify its
  desires — note *epithymia* returning from Lesson 17, now with an answer.

**Reflection**

- What provision am I currently making for something I claim to have renounced?

## Lesson 35 — To His Own Master He Stands

**Romans 14:1-12**

**Aim.** Apply ownership to disagreement between believers.

**Content**

- Welcome the one weak in faith, but not to quarrel over opinions.
- Who are you to pass judgment on the servant of another? **He stands or falls to his
  own master** — the *doulos* thread applied to how we treat each other.
- Each fully convinced in his own mind.
- Whether we live or die, we are the Lord's.
- We will all stand before the judgment seat of God; each gives account of himself.

**Reflection**

- Whose servant have I been judging?
- Where does "fully convinced in his own mind" make me uncomfortable, and why?

## Lesson 36 — Whatever Is Not from Faith

**Romans 14:13-23**

**Aim.** Weigh liberty against love, and end on a definition of sin that indicts
quietly.

**Content**

- Decide never to put a stumbling block in a brother's way.
- Nothing unclean in itself — but it is unclean for the one who thinks it is.
- Do not destroy by your food the one for whom Christ died.
- The kingdom is righteousness, peace and joy in the Holy Spirit.
- "Whatever does not proceed from faith is sin" — set beside Lesson 3's definition and
  see how far it reaches.

**Reflection**

- Where is my liberty costing someone else more than it gains me?
- What am I doing this week that does not proceed from faith?

## Lesson 37 — Bear with the Failings

**Romans 15:1-13**

**Aim.** Close the ethical section with Christ as the pattern, and Jew and Gentile
worshipping together.

**Content**

- The strong ought to bear the failings of the weak, not to please themselves.
- Christ did not please Himself — the pattern is His, not a strategy.
- The Scriptures written for our instruction, that we might have hope.
- Christ a servant to the circumcised to confirm the promises, and the Gentiles
  glorifying God for His mercy.
- The chain of Old Testament quotations, and the God of hope filling with joy and
  peace.

**Reflection**

- Am I using my strength to carry someone or to justify myself?

# Part Six: The Errand and the Household

*Romans 15:14-16:27 — the letter's own purpose, and the people who carry it.*

## Lesson 38 — Paul's Errand

**Romans 15:14-33**

**Aim.** See why this letter exists, and what apostolic ambition looks like.

**Content**

- Paul's boldness on some points by way of reminder, because of the grace given him.
- A priestly service of the gospel; the offering of the Gentiles.
- Ambition to preach where Christ has not been named.
- Spain, Jerusalem, the collection from Macedonia and Achaia.
- His request for their striving together with him in prayer.

**Reflection**

- Do I have any ambition shaped by the gospel rather than by preference?

## Lesson 39 — The Household

**Romans 16:1-16**

**Aim.** Let the names land; doctrine this heavy was addressed to actual people.

**Content**

- Phoebe commended, a servant of the church and a patron of many.
- Prisca and Aquila, who risked their necks; the church in their house.
- The sheer length of the list, and how many of them are women, relatives, fellow
  prisoners, the beloved.
- Greet one another with a holy kiss.

**Reflection**

- Would my name appear on someone's list like this? Whose would appear on mine?

## Lesson 40 — The Crushing and the Doxology

**Romans 16:17-27**

**Aim.** End where the letter ends: the Genesis 3 sentence executed, and God given
the last word.

**Content**

- Watch out for those causing divisions; they serve their own appetites.
- **The God of peace will soon crush Satan under your feet** — the Genesis 3 sentence,
  carried out, and carried out beneath the feet of the church. The vassal kings put
  back on duty.
- Final greetings from Paul's companions; Tertius, who wrote the letter.
- The doxology: the mystery now disclosed, the **obedience of faith** among all
  nations — the same phrase as Romans 1:5, closing the letter's frame.
- To the only wise God be glory forevermore.

**Reflection**

- Looking back over the whole letter: has anything moved between "I know this" and "I
  am resting on this"?
- The letter opens and closes on the obedience of faith. Which of those two words do I
  keep dropping?
